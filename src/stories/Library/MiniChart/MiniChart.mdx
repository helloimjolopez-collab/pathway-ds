The sparkline. A shape that gives a single number some history, small enough to
sit beside the number rather than under a heading of its own.

It is its own component page so that it can be found and composed with. It was
previously reachable only through KPI Tile, which meant anyone building a new
component had no way to know it existed.

**The shapes are real paths, not generated noise.** Figma draws twelve shapes and
seven of them are beziers. Sampling each at forty points and drawing the result
as a polyline gives a line with visible corners where the design has a curve: the
version that shipped first was described, accurately, as looking like sperm on
steroids. The implementation reads the `C` commands out of the Figma path to
decide which shapes are smooth, and smooths those with a Catmull-Rom spline
converted to cubic beziers at 1/6 tension, which passes through every sampled
point instead of near them.

## Anatomy

```
MiniChart
├── svg                      preserveAspectRatio="none", so it stretches
│   ├── area                 layered shapes only, fill at 10%
│   └── line                 stroke, sentiment-coloured
└── marker                   TWO HTML siblings, positioned in %
    ├── ring                 stroke at 20%
    └── dot                  solid
```

The marker is HTML rather than SVG for a reason that is not stylistic.
`preserveAspectRatio="none"` is what lets one path fill any box, and it stretches
circles into ellipses along with everything else. So the marker is positioned by
percentage and sized in pixels, outside the stretched coordinate space.

The ring and the dot are **siblings, not nested**. Nested opacity multiplies and
clamps at 1, so a dot inside a 20% ring can never reach full colour no matter
what opacity it is given. That is why the marker looked washed out at every size
before this was separated.

## States

<Canvas of={Stories.StateMatrix} />

A sparkline has no pointer states. What varies is its data and its sentiment, and
the states worth checking are the ones that break a line drawn naively: a flat
series, a single point, a shape that goes below its own start, and a layered
shape where the area has to render without a stroke or a marker.

<Canvas of={Stories.Shapes} />

All twelve shapes, each labelled with whether it is smooth. Compare any smooth
one against its polyline and the difference is the whole reason this component
was rebuilt.

## Token mappings

<Canvas of={Stories.TokensFill} />
<Canvas of={Stories.TokensStroke} />

Positive and Negative here are **sentiment, not direction**. A falling cost line
is Positive and is green while pointing down. The component takes `favourable`
separately from the shape for exactly this reason.

The area's 10% and the ring's 20% are applied to the fill and the stroke, never
to a wrapping group, for the nesting reason above.

## Responsive behaviour

The SVG stretches to its container on both axes, which is the point: a widget
that gets wider gets more line rather than more whitespace. The marker's size
does not scale, so it stays a readable dot at every width.

## Accessibility

| Concern | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| The shape | `aria-hidden="true"`. The number beside it carries the meaning, and a sparkline read aloud is noise. |
| Colour | Sentiment is also carried by the sign on the number and by the Change glyph, so the line's colour is never the only signal. |
| Reduced motion | Nothing animates, so there is nothing to suppress. |
