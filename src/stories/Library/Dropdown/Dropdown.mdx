A trigger and the menu it opens. Three trigger types, matching Figma's axis: an
icon, a button, and an avatar. The panel is Pathway's own `PopoverMenu`.

This component exists because a foreign dropdown had been nested inside the KPI
tiles and the widget headers. The panel's measurements are not invented: panel
212 wide at pad 8 and r=8, item 40 tall at pad 6 and 14, label gap 4 at an 8
inset, taken from `PopoverMenu` and `PopoverMenu.Item`.

`Open` is a Figma variant, which means it is a prop here rather than a CSS state.
That is what lets a story render an open menu for a screenshot without faking the
markup, and it is why the component accepts both a controlled `open` and an
uncontrolled default.

## Anatomy

```
Dropdown
├── trigger                 icon 20x20 | button | avatar 28
└── panel                   212 min, pad 8, r=8, --elevation-sheet
    ├── DropdownItem        40 tall, pad 6/14, r=8
    │   ├── leading glyph   14 in a 20 box, optional
    │   ├── label          14/500
    │   └── note           10, optional, trailing
    └── DropdownDivider    1px rule, pad 6 above and below
```

The item's horizontal padding is **14**, `Padding/Medium`. It read 12 until
2026-10-08 under a comment asserting that 14 was not on the ladder. That is true
of the gap ladder, which goes 12 then 16, and not of the padding ladder, which
has a Medium rung at exactly 14.

An item's hover changes its label and its fill together, within one token family:
`Foreground/Action/Secondary/Rest` to `/Hover`, with
`Fill/Action/Selection/Hover` behind it. A selection token rather than an action
one, because the row is a choice and not a button.

## States

<Canvas of={Stories.StateMatrix} />

Two things have states and they are separate components in Figma: the trigger,
whose `Open` axis is a variant, and the item, whose hover is its own. Treating
the panel's hover as a state of the trigger is how a menu ends up highlighting
the wrong row.

A checked row adds a leading check **without moving the label**, because a label
that shifts on selection reads as a different item. A disabled row does not take
the hover fill and is not focusable, so keyboard navigation skips it rather than
landing somewhere nothing can be chosen.

## In context

<Canvas of={Stories.InACard} />

The panel is an elevated surface rather than an overlay one, because it is
anchored to its trigger and does not dim what is behind it. Inside a card, that
means it sits on the card rather than over the page.

## Responsive behaviour

The panel is 212 at every width and aligns to its trigger's end edge by default,
flipping to the start edge when 212 would overflow the viewport. It does not
become a sheet on mobile: a menu of three or four actions is faster to hit where
the user's finger already is than at the bottom of the screen.

## Accessibility

| Concern | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| Trigger | `<button>` with `aria-haspopup="menu"` and `aria-expanded`. |
| Panel | `role="menu"`, items are `role="menuitem"`, checkable items carry `aria-checked`. |
| Keyboard | Enter or Space opens, Escape closes and returns focus to the trigger, Tab moves through items in authored order. |
| Disabled items | `disabled`, so they are skipped rather than focusable and inert. |
| Destructive items | Named by their label, not by colour alone. "Remove from this dashboard" reads the same without the red. |
